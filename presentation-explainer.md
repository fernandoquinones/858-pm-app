# The 858 Project Tool — the simple version

*A plain-language explainer for tomorrow. Read it top to bottom and you can present the whole thing without a single technical word.*

---

## The big idea, in one sentence
**You tell Claude about an event, it builds the whole project plan for you, and everyone works off the same living page — instead of passing a file back and forth.**

---

## The problem we have today
Right now our plan lives in a file we hand back and forth. The second two people open it, we have two versions. Someone changes a date, someone else doesn't see it, and now the plan is wrong. The plan works great — until more than one person touches it.

## The fix, as an analogy
Think of our current plan like a **paper menu** we photocopy and mail around. Everyone scribbles on their own copy, and no copy is fully right.

The new tool is like a **whiteboard on the wall that everyone can see at once.** One board. When anyone writes on it, everyone watching sees it instantly. There are no copies, so there's no "which one is right." And we hired a very fast assistant — Claude — who fills in the whole board for us the moment we describe the event.

---

## How it works — four steps
1. **You describe the event.** One sentence: "CFO luncheon, 20 execs, five tables, sponsor up front." That's it.
2. **Claude builds the plan.** It already knows every task we've ever run, so it writes out the full plan — tasks, who owns them, deadlines — automatically.
3. **The team works on one page.** Everybody — you, Christina, Nic, Juan — opens the same web link. **This is the main place to work: edit tasks, change dates, and leave comments right on the page.** It's always the latest; change something and everyone sees it within a second.
4. **Slack is a second way in — not the only one.** The folks who'd rather not open the tool get a Slack ping and can comment or tap ✅ to mark a task done from Slack. Anything done in Slack shows up on the webpage, and vice versa.

---

## Who can do what
- **Christina and Fern** run the show: create the plan with Claude, change anything, add new tasks or whole new workstreams whenever.
- **JG** builds the **seating chart** for assigned-seating events (luncheons, bird circles) — assign seats, swap, handle no-shows.
- **Nic, Juan, Chris, and Marty** can see the entire plan — every deadline and owner — and comment on anything, but they can only check off **their own** tasks. So nobody accidentally moves someone else's work.
- Everyone uses it in a **web browser** — that's where you edit and comment. Slack is just a convenient second door. Nothing to install.

---

## Why this is better than what we tried before
- **No more version confusion.** One page, always current. This was our single biggest headache.
- **No blank page.** Claude does the first 90% of the plan in seconds, from our own history.
- **Meets people where they are.** Power users get the full board; everyone else just gets a Slack nudge.
- **We can keep changing it.** Need a new workstream mid-event? Add it on the spot — no rebuild.

---

## What's real today vs. what's next
- **Working now (prototype):** the live plan, the seating board, Claude building plans from a sentence, roles, and comments — all saving to a real shared database.
- **Next:** the Slack notifications, a shareable web address for the whole team (so it's not just on my laptop), and proper logins.

---

## If someone asks "why not just buy Asana / Notion / Trello?"
We tried. None of them do what we actually need without a headache: they can't build a plan from one sentence using our own history, they don't bend to a new workstream on demand, and they make the casual folks log into yet another tool. This gives us the structure of a real tool *and* the flexibility of just telling Claude what we want.

---

## The one line to close on
**"We stopped emailing a file around. Now we describe the event, Claude builds the plan, and the whole team works off one live page — with Slack for everyone who'd rather not open it."**
